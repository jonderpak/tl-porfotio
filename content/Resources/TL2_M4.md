## Proposed JLJ Secondary LLC Circulation Policy (Grades 8–12)

### 1) Audience
- Students (grades 8–12) and all staff. Collections and timelines are set to balance recreational reading and course demands, consistent with the inclusive, curricular support role laid out in BC exemplars. 

### 2) Lending limits
- Books (Fiction/Non‑fiction): Up to 6 items at a time; 3‑week loan, 2 renewals if no holds. (JLJ currently lists 3‑week loans; we keep the 3‑week standard and increase the limit to expand equitable access.) 
- Graphic Novels & Manga: Up to 4 at a time; 2‑week loan, 1 renewal if no holds. (JLJ’s current 3‑day GN period is extended to improve access and belonging while still managing demand.)
- Magazines/Zines (if applicable): 2 at a time; 1‑week loan; no renewals (high turnover).
- Staff: No numeric limit; 6‑week loan, with semester‑length extensions for course sets if needed (supports instruction and planning, aligning with the library’s curricular mission). 

Rationale: Our limits aim to maximize equitable access and support curricular and recreational reading, a balance echoed in BC district guidelines. 

### 3) Circulation practices
- Self‑service friendly: Students can place holds, renew online, and request purchases; staff can request course‑aligned sets. (Builds transparency and trust per Pentland’s communication lens.)
- Privacy: We do not disclose a student’s reading history to others; communications go directly to the borrower (student or staff). (This aligns with professional norms around intellectual freedom and student dignity emphasized in current Canadian discourse.) 
- Challenged materials: Any concerns follow the posted reconsideration process (form + review steps), modeled on clear BC procedures. (Link from the circulation page.) 

### 4) Overdues
- Fine‑free for students and staff. Automated courtesy reminders (email/app) at 3, 7, and 14 days past due. After 21 days, item is “assumed lost” and billed (see below), but returns reverse the bill. (Shifts from punishment to communication and access—consistent with equity language in Canadian guidance and Pentland’s trust‑building approach.) 
### 5) Lost materials & fines
- Students: If an item is 30+ days overdue, a replacement charge appears in the student account. No punitive late fines. Return at any time waives the charge. If truly lost, families may: (a) pay replacement cost, or (b) provide an identical/new copy, or (c) meet with LLC staff for a case‑by‑case equity waiver. (Equity lens aligns with Canadian school library values for inclusive access.)
- Staff: Replacement cost invoiced at 45+ days, with the same options.

### 6) Holiday & break lending
- Extended loan windows begin one week before winter, spring, and summer breaks so students can keep reading and working on long‑term projects at home. High‑demand titles may be limited to 1 renewal to balance access. (Supports the “love of reading” and curricular goals highlighted in BC guidance and WVS’s policy language.)

## How this policy reflects the sources (brief mapping)
- Clarity + criteria prevent confusion: We publish a plain‑language “How borrowing works” page and link the reconsideration pathway, echoing Kirkland (language care) and WVS (documented process). 
- Communication beats punishment: Fine‑free + reminders + multiple renewal options reflect Pentland’s relationship‑first stance and Canadian guidance on equitable practices.
- Local fit: We keep JLJ’s 3‑week baseline for most books and improve graphic‑novel access from 3 days to 2 weeks, with holds/renewals to manage demand—maintaining JLJ’s student‑friendly tone while increasing belonging and fairness.

## Reflection prompts 
- **Belonging?**  
    Yes, higher student limits, fine‑free borrowing, extended breaks, and GN timelines signal trust and inclusion; the site’s friendly tone and purchase‑request options further invite participation (JLJ page style). 
- **Student privacy?**  
    Yes, no disclosure of reading histories; direct borrower communications; reconsideration goes through a formal process rather than ad‑hoc public debate (aligned with professional norms). 
- **Equitable access?**  
    Yes, fine‑free, multiple renewals, high‑demand controls (holds, shorter renewal window on hot titles), and extended holiday lending reduce access barriers; policy language centers fairness, echoing Canadian guidance. 
- **Mission & vision?**  
    Yes, our policy supports both curriculum and free reading and reflects UNESCO’s mission (as cited in WVS docs) to build lifelong learners and imaginative citizens. 
- **Maximize use for recreational + curricular?**  
    Yes, generous limits and renewals for novels/non‑fiction, better GN timelines, and staff flexibility for course sets ensure steady, fair circulation without unnecessary friction. (This harmonizes with BC policy exemplars emphasizing diverse, inclusive collections in service of learning.)
